## 3. Assumptions the sandbox run must confirm

Each assumption has a test against the fake now and a line under "Sin verificar" in `docs/done/4.4.md`.

- **A1** `v1` is lowercase hex of the HMAC. `t` is Unix seconds. One header may carry several `v1` values, and any match is accepted. If Pin uses base64, only the decoder changes (one test).
- **A2** The endpoint's `key` (as returned by the API and shown in the dashboard) is the signing key.
- **A3** A webhook body is the Events object `{ token, type, data, created_at }` (no `response` wrapper), with `data` the charge or refund as the API returns it.
- **A4** A test-host call with a live key, or with any unknown key, answers 401.
- **A5** `GET /charges/{token}` gives the final state of a 3-D Secure charge after the customer finishes at the ACS, even if we never call `verify` (customer closed the tab). If false, a charge whose callback was lost stays `pending` until the expiry task (§6.8).
- **A6** An abandoned 3-D Secure charge ends unsuccessful at Pin within hours, and never succeeds later without the customer.
- **A7** A declined charge answers 400 with one of the listed codes, and a decline leaves no charge that can still succeed.
- **A8** Charge `metadata` is copied to the webhook `data` and is searchable at once (`query=<value>` finds the charge within seconds of the create answer).
- **A9** Refunds accept no metadata (we send none). If they do accept it, a later change can add our id. Our id comes from our store meanwhile (§7).
- **A10** A refund appears in `GET /charges/{token}/refunds` as soon as the create call has answered or timed out at Pin.
- **A11** The 3-D Secure `redirect_url` is always under `https://sandbox.checkout.com` in test mode. Any other host is refused (§6.4), so a wrong guess blocks payment but never redirects a customer somewhere unexpected.
- **A12** The Hosted Fields iframes and their tokenisation calls come from `https://cdn.pinpayments.com` and the API host of the mode (the CSP of §10.3). If wrong, the card page shows no fields. Only the CSP list changes.
- **A13** Pin sends the payer no email by default. The guide tells Oscar to check this and turn receipts off, because our receipts (4.6) are the ones sent.
- **A14** `GET /balance` needs no permission beyond the secret key, moves nothing and returns no personal data. It serves as the authenticated probe of §5.2.
- **A15** With `fallback_ok: false`, a card that is not enrolled is refused (400 or 422 `three_d_secure_unavailable`), and nothing is charged.

## 4. Architecture: card sessions in the core, the adapter only talks to Pin

### 4.1 The mismatch

Stripe and PayPal create an object at the gateway when we call `createPayment`, so the customer pays on the gateway's page and cancel is a gateway call. Pin has no such object. A charge needs a card token, and the token exists only after the customer has typed the card into Pin's iframes on our page. Between "we start a payment" and "the customer submits a card", the state (open, cancelled, expired, how many declines) has to live with us. An adapter is stateless and may not touch Prisma.

### 4.2 The design

- **`adapters/pin_payments/`** implements a narrow, gateway-neutral interface `CardTokenGateway` (new, in `core/card-sessions/gateway.ts`): `probe`, `charge(token, …)`, `verifyAuthentication(sessionToken)`, `retrieveCharge`, `searchCharges(metadataValue)`, `createRefund`, `retrieveRefund`, `listRefunds(chargeToken)`, `testConnection`, `verifyWebhook`, `parseEvent`, `signatureHeaders`. The isolation rules apply as for every adapter (`adapter_sources_are_isolated`): imports only `core/provider`, `core/card-sessions/gateway` (types) and `node:crypto`. No environment, logs or Prisma.
- **`core/card-sessions/provider.ts`**: `CardSessionProvider implements PaymentProvider`, built from a `CardTokenGateway` and a `CardSessionStore`. This is what `createAdapters` registers under the key `pin_payments`. It owns the pre-card state and the refund intents (§7), and maps everything to `ProviderPayment` and `ProviderRefund`. It also has two members only the card-session service calls: `submitCard` (§6.3) and `finishAuthentication` (§6.4).
- **`CardSessionStore`**: a port with two implementations. `PrismaCardSessionStore` (tables of §11) is for the API. `MemoryCardSessionStore` is for the contract subject and unit tests. Every change is a compare-and-set on `(id, version)`.
- **`core/card-sessions/card-sessions.service.ts` and `.controller.ts`**: the customer endpoints of §10.1. They lock the attempt, check that it is live, the booking is payable and the account is `active`, then call `submitCard` through `ProviderGateway.call(accountId, 'create', …)`. So paused, retired, live-outside-production and unverified-live are all refused by the existing guard matrix, and the circuit breaker counts submits as creates.

### 4.3 Why this keeps the contract unchanged

The contract runs against the registered `PaymentProvider`, so here against `CardSessionProvider(PinAdapter(fake transport), MemoryCardSessionStore)`. `CreatePaymentInput` is untouched, so `create_input_has_no_card_fields` holds as written: the card token reaches only `submitCard`, never `createPayment`. The subject's `customerCompletes` is "the customer submits a card on our page": fake tokenisation, then `submitCard`, then the fake 3-D Secure if asked. That is what a subject is for ("how a customer pays comes from the subject"). No clause is edited, skipped or made conditional.

The design is reusable. Any later hosted-fields gateway (eWAY Secure Fields, Fat Zebra, Windcave drop-in) implements `CardTokenGateway` and gets sessions, the card page and the contract for free.

## 5. Manifest, credentials, HTTP

### 5.1 Manifest

- `key: 'pin_payments'`, `displayName: 'Pin Payments'`, `status: 'experimental'`, `testOnly: false`, `cardDataHandling: 'hosted_only'`.
- `methods: ['card']`: Apple Pay and Google Pay through Pin need domain verification, out of scope. `currencies: ['AUD']`, `countries: 'any'`.
- `checkoutOrigin`: the card page's origin, which is `WEB_ORIGIN`, passed in `AdapterOptions` as for the simulator. `manifestProblems` gets one rule. An adapter with `cardFields` (below) must have `https` outside `development`, `test` and `ci`. Inside them, the simulator's `TEST_ONLY_ORIGIN` shape applies, so `http://localhost:3000` works in `pnpm dev`. Production already requires an `https` `WEB_ORIGIN` (`scripts/check-deploy-config.mjs`).
- New optional manifest member `cardFields`, read by the card page and the CSP. Its values are fixed in code, and only the mode selects between them:

  ```ts
  cardFields?: { scriptUrl: string; frameOrigins: readonly string[]; challengeOrigins: { test: readonly string[]; live: readonly string[] } };
  ```

  Pin: `scriptUrl: 'https://cdn.pinpayments.com/pin.hosted_fields.v1.js'`, `frameOrigins: ['https://cdn.pinpayments.com']` (A12), `challengeOrigins.test: ['https://sandbox.checkout.com']` (A11), `challengeOrigins.live: []` (unreachable while `experimental`; empty means nothing live is ever followed). `manifestProblems` requires `https` origins without paths and `scriptUrl` under one of `frameOrigins`.
- `capabilities: { refunds: 'partial', deferredCapture: false, lookupByIdempotencyKey: true, declineOutcome: 'stays_payable' }`. A declined card leaves the session open for another card (§6.5). No `disputes` (§9), no `reconciliation` (§9).

### 5.2 Credentials (4.1 account setup)

- `secretKey`: label "Secret API key (test account)", pattern `^[A-Za-z0-9_-]{16,128}$`. Pin keys carry no documented test or live marker, so as with PayPal the host keeps a test account on test money: a test context only ever calls `test-api.pinpayments.com` (A4).
- `publishableKey`: label "Publishable API key (test account)", same pattern, and a new optional field flag `exposure: 'card_page'` (D4). Pin designs this key for the browser (`tokenize({ publishable_api_key })`). It is stored encrypted like every field and never returned by the panel's API. The only place it leaves the server is the card-session view of §10.1, for that session's account, while the session is open. `manifestProblems` allows `exposure` only on adapters with `cardFields`, and at most one such field. A test checks that the panel's responses, the audit log and the logs never contain it, and that the card-session view contains only it and no other field.
- `webhookSecret`: `{ source: 'entered', label: 'Signing key of the webhook endpoint', pattern: '^[A-Za-z0-9_-]{16,64}$' }` (A2: the example `key` has 22 characters).
- Samples are built at runtime from filler, never a key-shaped literal. Rejected samples: values with spaces, `.`, `:`, `=`, too short, too long.
- `testConnection`: `GET /balance` (auth), then, with a webhook key set, `GET /webhook_endpoints` (at most 5 endpoints, documented), and one endpoint's `key` must equal `ctx.webhookSecret`, compared with `timingSafeEqual` on equal-length buffers. No key: `webhook_secret_missing`. No endpoint with that key: `provider_rejected` (as PayPal does for a webhook that is missing). Never throws.

### 5.3 HTTP layer

- Hosts fixed in code by `ctx.mode`: test `https://test-api.pinpayments.com/1`, live `https://api.pinpayments.com/1` (unreachable while `experimental`). No option or variable changes them. A unit test checks that a test context never reaches another host.
- Each request: `fetch` with `redirect: 'error'`, a 4.5 s timeout (8 s for `POST /charges`, which Pin calls long-running, still inside the wrapper's 10 s), JSON in and out (`application/x-www-form-urlencoded` is not used), body capped at 1 MiB, else `provider_unavailable`.
- Errors are read only for `error` (a code), compared with closed lists. Pin's `error_description` and `messages` never leave the adapter. 401 and 403 mean `provider_auth_failed`, non-retryable. 404 means `null` or `provider_not_found` as the member requires. 409, 429 and 5xx mean `provider_busy` or `provider_unavailable`, retryable. A timeout means `provider_timeout`, retryable.
- Tokens are checked before they are put in a URL: `^ch_[A-Za-z0-9_-]{10,40}$`, `^rf_…`, `^dis_…`, `^se_…`. Malformed ones never reach Pin. They are checked after the probe, so bad credentials still give `provider_auth_failed` (contract `bad_credentials_are_auth_failed`).
- **Probe (A14).** Every `CardSessionProvider` operation that would otherwise answer from the store alone (create, retrieve of a session without a charge, cancel of an open session, refund lookups) first calls `gateway.probe()` (`GET /balance`). So bad credentials are `provider_auth_failed` everywhere, and an account Pin no longer accepts never shows a card page. No cache (as PayPal, D7 of 4.3).
- Secret key, publishable key, webhook key, email and IP never appear in a `ProviderError`, a result or a log (C20 and a unit test).

## 6. Payments

### 6.1 Money

- Only `AUD` (manifest). Anything else is refused before any call or store write (`provider_rejected`, definitive: `definitive_refusal_means_no_object`).
- `1n ≤ amount ≤ 999_999_999n`, else refused before any call. Pin takes the integer as is: `amount` in a JSON number built from a `bigint` checked to be ≤ 2^53 − 1 (`Number(x)` only after that check, in one function with a test). Answers are read with a strict integer check (`Number.isSafeInteger`), then `BigInt`. No floats and no arithmetic on them. A static test greps the adapter for `parseFloat`, `toFixed` and `*`/`/` next to amounts.
- No rounding: the amount arrives in minor units from the core. The rounding rule of `.claude/rules/pricing.md` is not involved.

### 6.2 Create: a session, no money

`createPayment(ctx, input)`: currency and amount checks, probe, then `store.createOrGet(accountId, idempotencyKey, { amount, paymentId, bookingId, returnUrl, createdAt: input.createdAt })`:

- New: a session with `ref = 'cs_' + 32 random hex` (a bearer value, like a Stripe Checkout URL), `status: 'open'`, `expiresAt = input.createdAt + 90 min` (the core's attempt expires at 60 min and cancels first; the 30 min margin lets that cancel find the session open).
- Existing (same key): returned as stored, whatever amount is asked now, so a key never gives a second object (`same_key_other_amount_never_a_second_object`). The core's amount check sees the difference.
- Answer: `requires_action`, `nextAction: { type: 'redirect', url: `${checkoutOrigin}/pay/card/${ref}` }`.

### 6.3 Submit a card: `submitCard`

Called only by the card-session service (§10.1), never by sync, drive, expire, cancel, replace, orphans or refunds.

1. The service locks the payment (attempt) row, then checks: the attempt is live (`requires_action`) and its ref is this session's, the booking is payable and not under review (`bookingUnderPaymentReview`), and the account is `active`. Otherwise it answers 409 and nothing is called.
2. Store compare-and-set `open → submitting` (with `declines < 5` and `now < expiresAt`). If it fails, 409 `card_session_not_open`. Exactly one submit can be in flight per session: a double click, two tabs or a replayed request cannot create two charges.
3. Outside any transaction, via `ProviderGateway.call(accountId, 'create', …)`: `POST /charges` with `amount`, `currency: 'AUD'`, `description: 'Booking payment'`, `email` (the payer's, read by the service from the booking), `ip_address` (the request's client address as resolved by the platform's trusted-proxy rule), `card_token`, `capture: true`, `metadata: { mt_session: ref, mt_payment: paymentId }`, `three_d_secure: { enabled: true, fallback_ok: false, callback_url: `${checkoutOrigin}/api/payments/card-sessions/${ref}/authenticated` }` (D6, A15). No booking reference, no name, no description with personal data.
4. Outcomes, each a compare-and-set from `submitting`:

| Pin answer | Session | Card page |
|---|---|---|
| 201, `success: true` | `succeeded`, `chargeRef` | goes to `returnUrl` |
| 202 with `redirect_url` under `challengeOrigins[mode]` | `authenticating`, `chargeRef` | goes to `redirect_url` |
| 202 with any other host, or no `redirect_url` | `authenticating`, `chargeRef` | "We could not start your bank's check" (A11). The charge ends at Pin (A6) and the sweep reads it |
| 400 decline code, or 422 `three_d_secure_unavailable` | `open`, `declines + 1`, last `failureCode` from a closed list (`card_declined`, `insufficient_funds`, `expired_card`, `card_not_authenticated`, `processing_error`) | "Your card was declined. Try another card." After the fifth: `cancelled` with `too_many_declines` |
| 400 `token_already_used` | `submitting` → resolved as "unknown" below | — |
| 422 `invalid_resource` | `cancelled`, `failureCode: 'provider_rejected'` (definitive, nothing charged) | "This payment cannot be completed" |
| 401/403 | `open` again (nothing charged); `provider_auth_failed` reaches the breaker | error |
| Timeout, 5xx, network | stays `submitting` (unknown) | "We are checking your payment" |

5. **Unknown outcome.** A session in `submitting` is resolved only by reading Pin: `GET /charges/search?query=<ref>` (A8). One charge with `metadata.mt_session === ref` binds it as above. More than one is impossible by step 2. If it happens, the answer is `provider_unavailable` (unknown, never none) and a task. None found keeps it `submitting` (`pending` to the core, which blocks a second attempt, P2). An unknown session **never goes back to `open`**. If nothing appears by `expiresAt`, it becomes `cancelled` with `outcome_unknown`. A charge that shows up later is a late success, which the core already turns into an overpayment and a task (`late_success_of_cancelled_attempt_becomes_overpayment`, P2). The cost of this rule: a customer whose submit timed out starts a new payment instead of retrying the same one. It can never cost a second charge.

### 6.4 3-D Secure: `finishAuthentication`

`GET /api/payments/card-sessions/:ref/authenticated?session_token=se_…`. Pin sends the customer there "regardless of outcome".

- `session_token` must match `^se_[A-Za-z0-9_-]{10,64}$`. Otherwise no call is made, and the browser goes to `returnUrl`.
- The session must be `authenticating`, else 303 to `returnUrl` and nothing else.
- `GET /charges/verify?session_token=…`. The charge's `token` must equal the session's `chargeRef`, else `reference_mismatch`: nothing is recorded and the response is a task. `success: true` sets `succeeded`. `success: false` sets `open` with `declines + 1` and `card_not_authenticated` (the customer may try another card). Still pending leaves the session unchanged. 403, 404 and 422 leave it unchanged. Then 303 to `returnUrl`, where the existing return page refreshes the attempt (`sync`).
- The redirect target is always the session's stored `returnUrl` (built by the core, §6.2), never a parameter.

### 6.5 Statuses (session → `ProviderPayment`)

| Session | Ours |
|---|---|
| `open` (before `expiresAt`) | `requires_action`, redirect to the card page, `failureCode` of the last decline if any |
| `submitting` | `pending` |
| `authenticating` | `pending` while Pin's charge is pending (read on every retrieve); its final state is applied first |
| `succeeded` | `succeeded`; the amount is the charge's `amount` as Pin reports it |
| `cancelled`, or `open` past `expiresAt` | `cancelled` (past `expiresAt` the store is moved to `cancelled` with `expired` on the read) |

There is no `failed`: a decline leaves the session payable (`declineOutcome: 'stays_payable'`). Its terminal ends are `succeeded` and `cancelled`. In the contract, `endAtGateway` moves the memory store's clock past `expiresAt`. `pending` from `authenticating` blocks a new attempt (P2). The abandoned case is in §6.8.

`ourPaymentId` comes from the store, or from `metadata.mt_payment` when the charge has no session (a charge made in Pin's dashboard).

### 6.6 Retrieve, lookup, cancel

- `retrievePayment({ ref })`: a `cs_` ref reads the store. Unknown means `null` after the probe. With a `chargeRef` (and in `authenticating`, or `succeeded` when asked), `GET /charges/{chargeRef}` is read and any final state is applied to the store first. A `ch_` ref (the `paymentRef` of a refund event) uses the store's index on `chargeRef`. Without a session, it uses the charge itself (`ref` = the charge token, `ourPaymentId` from metadata), so `refund_webhook_verifies_and_resolves_payment` and `gateway_refund_has_no_our_id` resolve to the session's ref. Read only: never charges, captures or verifies.
- `retrievePayment({ idempotencyKey })`: the store by `(accountId, key)`, then as above. Exact, with no time window. Unknown means `null` after the probe (`retrieve_unknown_is_null`).
- `cancelPayment(ref)`: unknown ref means `provider_not_found` after the probe. `open` becomes `cancelled` (compare-and-set). `submitting` is resolved first (§6.3 step 5). If still unknown, `provider_busy` (retryable: P2 never replaces without the gateway's answer). `authenticating` reads the charge: final succeeded means `succeeded` is reported, final unsuccessful means `cancelled`, still pending means `provider_busy`. `succeeded` and `cancelled` are reported as they are. Idempotent: the key argument is unused, because a compare-and-set on the store needs none.
- A cancelled session cannot be paid: `submitCard` needs `open` (§6.3 step 2), and the card page shows "This payment is no longer open". That is the contract's `cancel_is_idempotent_and_final`, held by our own state rather than by trusting the gateway.

### 6.7 No surcharge, amount from the server

The card page shows and charges exactly the session's amount, which `createPayment` received from the core (P1). Hosted Fields do not surcharge; the Payment Page, which can, is not used. `no_surcharge_by_payment_method (task 4.4)`: the same booking routed to the simulator and to Pin gives the same amount on the card page and in the charge sent to the fake.

### 6.8 Sweeps and known limits

- The core's existing expiry and sync paths call `retrievePayment` and `cancelPayment`. They resolve `submitting` and `authenticating` as above. No new job.
- An abandoned 3-D Secure (customer left the bank's page) keeps the attempt `pending` until Pin ends the charge (A6). Unlike the simulator, the attempt cannot be replaced at once (`abandoned_3ds_resumed_or_replaced_immediately` is a simulator property). The card page says so. If Pin never ends it, the existing 24 h stuck-attempt task reaches Oscar, as for PayPal in 4.3.
- If A5 is false and the callback is lost, the same applies.

## 7. Refunds

Pin documents neither idempotency nor metadata for refunds (A9). The store keeps a **refund intent** per `(accountId, idempotencyKey)`: `refundId`, `chargeRef`, amount, `createdAt` and status `submitting` / `bound` / `refused`, plus `refundRef`.

- `createRefund(input)`: currency check. `paymentRef` must resolve to a session `succeeded` with a `chargeRef`, or a charge, else `provider_rejected` (definitive, `refund_of_unpaid_payment_refused`). `GET /charges/{chargeRef}`: refused without a call (definitive) unless `success && captured`, `amount > 0` and `amount ≤ charge.amount − charge.amount_refunded − (pending refunds listed)` (`refund_over_amount_refused`). Then compare-and-set insert of the intent (an existing intent: next point). Then `POST /charges/{chargeRef}/refunds { amount }`, always with the amount (never the empty body that means "everything"). The answer binds `refundRef`. 402 `insufficient_pin_balance` and other 4xx set `refused` (definitive, nothing exists). Timeout or 5xx leaves `submitting`.
- An existing intent with `refundRef`: `GET /refunds/{refundRef}` (the retry answers the same refund, `refund_full_and_idempotent`). An existing `submitting` intent: `GET /charges/{chargeRef}/refunds`, candidates with the same amount, `created_at ≥ intent.createdAt − 120 s` and not bound to another intent. Exactly one binds it. More than one gives `provider_unavailable` and a task. None gives `provider_unavailable`, retryable (A10). **It is never posted twice**: an unknown outcome is never retried with a second `POST`. 4.5's stuck-refund sweep and "resolve" path reach Oscar.
- `retrieveRefund({ ref })`: `GET /refunds/{ref}`. 404 means `null`. `ourRefundId` comes from the intent bound to that ref, so a refund we started carries our id (`refund_retrieve_by_ref_and_key_agree`) and a dashboard refund carries none (`gateway_refund_has_no_our_id`). `retrieveRefund({ idempotencyKey, paymentRef })` reads the intent, then as above.
- Statuses: `status_message` `Pending` means `pending`, `Success` means `succeeded`. `Failed`, or `error_message` set with `success: false`, means `failed`. Any other value is `provider_unavailable` (we cannot read it). `paymentRef` is the refund's `charge` token, which `retrievePayment` resolves to the session ref (§6.6).
- Refund events read in `parseEvent` carry no `ourRefundId` (it is pure, and Pin has no metadata). The core matches them by `providerRefundRef`, which it stores when `createRefund` answers. **Residual race:** an event processed before that answer is stored is unmatched. Implementation checks first whether the core already re-reads the refund with `retrieveRefund({ ref })` before classifying it as `external` (the intent then gives `ourRefundId`). If it does not, one small, adapter-neutral core change is added there: before creating an `external` refund, call `retrieveRefund({ ref })` and use its `ourRefundId` when present. Test: `webhook_before_provider_response_matches_app_refund (pin)`.
- Refunds on paused and retired accounts work as for every adapter (4.1, `retired_account_refunds_but_rejects_new_charges` extended to Pin).

## 8. Webhooks

### 8.1 Verification (synchronous, no network)

1. Header `pin-signature`, at most 512 characters, ASCII. It is parsed as comma-separated `k=v` parts: exactly one `t` (`^[0-9]{1,12}$`) and one to four `v1` (`^[0-9a-f]{64}$`, A1). Missing, oversized or other shapes give `bad_signature` (`malformed` only for a body that is not an event). Never throws.
2. An empty `ctx.webhookSecret` gives `bad_signature`.
3. The expected value is HMAC-SHA256(`ctx.webhookSecret`, `${t}.` + raw body bytes), compared with each `v1` by `timingSafeEqual` on 32-byte buffers. No match gives `bad_signature`.
4. `|now − t| > 300 s` either way gives `stale_timestamp`, checked after the signature so only genuine deliveries get that code (contract: 299 s, 301 s, 1 h).
5. Body: JSON object with `token` (`^evt_[A-Za-z0-9_-]{10,40}$`), `type` (≤ 64), `created_at` (strict ISO 8601) and `data` an object. Otherwise `malformed`, with `claimedEventId` when `token` is readable. Body size: the core's 256 KiB limit (P6) applies before.

`signatureHeaders: ['pin-signature']`, stored without their value. Webhook URLs in Pin's examples carry a `signature` query parameter. We ignore it: only the header scheme above is accepted (an older scheme would fail closed, then show in A1's sandbox check).

### 8.2 Events (`parseEvent`, pure)

| Pin `type` | `kind` | Mapping |
|---|---|---|
| `charge.captured`, `charge.authorised` | `payment` | `data` = charge. `ref` = `metadata.mt_session` when it has the `cs_` shape, else the charge token. Status from the charge (`success && captured` gives `succeeded`; otherwise `pending`). `ourPaymentId` = `metadata.mt_payment` when a UUID |
| `charge.failed` | `payment`, `requires_action` | The read decides (§6.5): a decline leaves the session open |
| `charge.voided` | `payment`, `requires_action` | Read decides. We never authorise, so this is not expected |
| `refund.created`, `refund.succeeded`, `refund.failed` | `refund` | `data` = refund; status from `status_message` (§7), not from the event name |
| `dispute.*` | `unsupported`, `review: 'dispute'` | Stored raw, task for Oscar (§9) |
| anything else | `unsupported` | Stored raw, not processed |

`providerEventId` = `token`. Duplicates share it, and the core stores one per account (P5, P6). Out of order: statuses come from `data`, the core's state machines only move forward, and when in doubt the core reads the gateway (`retrievePayment` resolves through the store), so a late `charge.failed` after `charge.captured` changes nothing. Raw bodies are stored by the core before processing, as for every adapter. They contain the payer's email, IP and the card's masked number and name as Pin sends them. They are dropped when the customer is erased (5.5), as for PayPal.

### 8.3 Events and the store

`parseEvent` never touches the store. When the core processes a payment event, it matches by `ourPaymentId` among this account's payments and then calls `retrievePayment`, which applies the charge to the session (§6.6). So a charge event for a `submitting` session (lost answer) binds it through the normal path, with no special case.

## 9. Disputes and reconciliation: not declared in 4.4

- **Disputes.** Pin has a Disputes API, but how the funds move (`chargeback_recovery` ledger entries, timing, reinstatement) is not documented in what was read. Wrong funds flags would post wrong ledger entries. So, as PayPal did in 4.3: no `capabilities.disputes`, no `retrieveDispute`. `dispute.*` events are `unsupported` with `review: 'dispute'`, so the core opens a task, and the dispute clauses check that nothing dispute-like is claimed. `docs/done/4.4.md` lists the mapping a follow-up would use once the sandbox shows real objects: status `evidence_required`, `evidence_under_review`, `won`, `lost`, `expired`, `accepted`, `cancelled`, `arbitration_*`, and funds from the activity feed.
- **Reconciliation.** No `capabilities.reconciliation`: 4.6 compares our objects one by one. `GET /charges`, `/refunds`, `/disputes` and Deposits could back `listActivity` and `listSettlements` later. Out of scope.

## 10. Customer endpoints and the card page

### 10.1 API (`core/card-sessions/card-sessions.controller.ts`)

All `@Public()` (the session ref is the bearer), `Cache-Control: no-store` and `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`. Unknown, malformed (`^cs_[0-9a-f]{32}$`) or foreign-mode refs get the same 404 as an unknown route.

- `GET /payments/card-sessions/:ref` returns `{ status: 'open' | 'processing' | 'finished', amountMinor, currency, card?: { publishableKey, sandbox: mode === 'test', scriptUrl }, declined?: <closed code> }`. `card` is present only while `open` and the account is `active`. No booking reference, name, email, account id or other credential. The amount is the session's, set by the core (the web displays it and computes nothing).
- `POST /payments/card-sessions/:ref/submit` takes `{ cardToken }`, with a strict schema (`^card_[A-Za-z0-9_-]{10,64}$`). Any other field (number, cvc, expiry, amount, URL) is a 422 and nothing is written (`card_data_never_reaches_api` extended). It returns `{ next: 'redirect', url }` (3-D Secure, or `returnUrl` on success) or `{ next: 'retry', declined }` or `{ next: 'wait' }`. Rate limits (card testing): 5 declines per session (§6.3), 10 submits per IP per 10 min, 30 per account per 10 min (the existing guard pattern of `rate-limit.guard.ts`). Over the limit gives 429 and Pin is not called. CSRF: same-origin `fetch` from the card page, through the existing CSRF guard as for checkout.
- `GET /payments/card-sessions/:ref/authenticated` (§6.4) always answers 303 to the session's `returnUrl`, or to the generic return page if the ref is unknown. It never redirects to a parameter.

### 10.2 Web: `apps/web/src/app/pay/card/[ref]/page.tsx`

- Dynamic, `noindex`, no ISR (a payment page, like checkout). Server component fetches the view. Small client component: loads `scriptUrl` once, `HostedFields.create({ sandbox, fields: { name, number, cvc, expiry } })`, on submit calls `tokenize({ publishable_api_key })`, posts only the token, then follows `url` with `window.location.assign` (only `https` URLs, or same-origin; the API already checked the challenge host).
- No input of ours ever holds card data: the four containers are empty `div`s that Pin fills with iframes. A static test fails if the page or component declares an `input` or `name` for number, cvc, expiry or name, and a component test checks the posted body is `{ cardToken }` only.
- Copy in `messages/en-AU.json` (amount label, declined, processing, not open, bank check, start again). Brand from `brand.config.ts`. No claims of accreditation or "official".
- WCAG 2.2 AA: visible labels outside the iframes, errors in an `aria-live` region, the submit button disabled only while a request is in flight, keyboard order through the iframes, focus visible, 24 px targets, contrast tokens. Unit tests with a mocked `HostedFields` global (no network in CI).

### 10.3 CSP for that route only

In `apps/web/src/proxy.ts`, the route `/pay/card/*` gets its own nonce-based CSP: `script-src 'nonce-…' 'strict-dynamic' https://cdn.pinpayments.com`, `frame-src https://cdn.pinpayments.com` (A12), `connect-src 'self'`, `form-action 'self'`, `frame-ancestors 'none'`, `base-uri 'none'`, `object-src 'none'`. The origins come from one shared constant in `packages/shared`, which the manifest also uses, so the two cannot drift (a test compares them). Every other route's CSP is unchanged. That is the "wider `frame-src`" plan 8.5 anticipated, limited to this route.

### 10.4 PCI

An iframe page keeps card data out of our systems. Which self-assessment Oscar's acquirer will ask for with a page that hosts the fields (instead of a redirect) is not confirmed. It goes to "Para la revisión de Oscar" with a question for Pin. Nothing in this design stores, logs or forwards card data. The card token is single-use and is not stored.

## 15. Decisions taken without Oscar

- **D1** Pin Payments over eWAY (§1).
- **D2** The pre-card state lives in a core decorator with its own store (§4). The adapter stays stateless and the contract stays unchanged.
- **D3** Hosted Fields on our card page, not Pin's Payment Page (amount in the URL, surcharge).
- **D4** The publishable key is a credential field flagged `exposure: 'card_page'`, returned only by the card-session view of an open session.
- **D5** `capture: true`. The gate is our session plus the core's live, payable and active check.
- **D6** 3-D Secure always on, `fallback_ok: false` (conservative on fraud and disputes; may refuse some unenrolled cards). Reversible in one constant.
- **D7** A submit with an unknown outcome never reopens the session.
- **D8** A refund with an unknown outcome is never posted again.
- **D9** Five declines per session, then it is cancelled. Rate limits per IP and per account.
- **D10** Payer email and IP are sent to Pin (required by Pin) and nowhere else. They are not stored in our tables; raw webhook bodies keep what Pin sends, erasable as today.
- **D11** No disputes and no reconciliation capabilities in 4.4.
- **D12** Session lifetime 90 minutes from the attempt row's creation.
- **D13** `methods: ['card']` only.
