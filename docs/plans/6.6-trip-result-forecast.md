## 2. Money rules shared by the three parts

1. **Integer minor units, always.** Every sum is a `bigint` sum in SQL or TypeScript; there is no `Number`, no float, no rate multiplication. Amounts travel as strings of digits. The browser only formats (the existing `formatMoney`).
2. **Per currency, never mixed.** Every figure belongs to one currency. A trip is `(package, currency)` where currency is the package's. Costs in another currency are listed in their own currency and are **never** converted, summed with, or netted against the trip's.
3. **The one exception: a conversion staff typed.** 6.2 lets staff record, from the bank statement, what the bank debited (`baseAmountMinor` in `baseCurrency`) for a payment in the supplier's currency. When a payment's items all belong to one trip and its `baseCurrency` is that trip's currency, both amounts are facts staff entered; the trip result uses the bank amount for those items (section 3.4). Nothing else is converted. A payment that covers several trips, or whose bank currency is a third one, stays in the other-currency list.
4. **Implied rate (finding 3).** The rate between the two typed amounts is shown for information and is never an input to any amount: `impliedRateE9(a, ca, b, cb)` is the number of major units of `cb` per one major unit of `ca`, times 10^9, worked out with the currencies' decimal places (`currencyExponent`: JPY 0, AUD 2, KWD 3, from the platform's own currency data, as `format.ts` does) as `round_half_up(b * 10^ea * 10^9 / (a * 10^eb))` on `bigint`. This is the single rounding rule of the task, stated and tested once; no other step rounds. Test `fx_conversion_respects_currency_exponents`: 10 000 JPY for 100.00 AUD is 0.01 AUD per JPY (`10_000_000`), and 100.00 AUD for 10 000 JPY is 100 JPY per AUD (`100_000_000_000`); the naive ratio of minor units would say 1. A zero or negative amount is refused.
5. **The `fx_conversion` read.** Per environment mode, per (supplier currency, bank currency) pair, over `recorded` supplier payments that carry a bank amount: the sum of supplier-currency amounts, the sum of bank amounts, the number of payments, and the implied average rate of the two sums (rule 4; information only). Cross-check with the ledger: for each currency K, the net of `fx_conversion` ledger entries in K equals (sum of bank amounts where K is the bank currency) minus (sum of supplier amounts where K is the supplier currency), and the response says whether it matches (`fx_read_matches_ledger`). Voided payments are not counted by either side because their reversal mirrors them.
6. **Environment mode only.** Everything reads rows of the environment's `PaymentMode` (plan 0.1 P11), in one `REPEATABLE READ` read-only transaction, like 4.6. A test-mode row never appears in production.
7. **Dates are Brisbane calendar dates** (`todayInBrisbane`; a day ends at `D 14:00:00Z`). Tests at `13:59:59.999Z` and `14:00:00.000Z`.
8. **No PII.** Nothing here reads a name, email, free-text field (`reference`, `voidReason`, cost `description`, refund `reason`) or payer detail. Bookings appear by their random reference only in the ledger dataset of the export.
9. **Every figure comes from the server.** Web and CSV show what the API computed.

## 3. Result per trip

`GET /admin/payments/reports/trip-result` (permission `payments.report.tripResult`). Pure core in `core/trip-result.ts` (table-tested), service `core/trip-result.service.ts` (SQL reads in one snapshot, then the pure function).

### 3.1 Inputs per booking (live ledger, as 4.6)

Per booking of the trip (non-demo is not filtered: the ledger decides, as 4.6): status, return date, `totalMinor`, and from `ledger_entries` of the mode in the booking's currency: `funds` (credit balance of `customer_funds`), `over` (`customer_overpayments`), `receivable` (debit balance of `customer_receivable`), `fees` (debit balance of `payment_fees`). Bookings are included with a `LEFT JOIN`: a live booking with no money yet still counts for the expected revenue. Cancellation (one per booking): `penaltyMinor`. Refunds: `succeeded` refunds of the booking, by purpose.

### 3.2 Definitions, per trip and currency (all `bigint`, trip currency)

Let `max0(x)` clamp at 0 per booking (a debt never offsets another booking, as 4.6 R2). `earned(b)` = status `cancelled`, `refunded` or `completed`, or return date before today in Brisbane. A trip is `settled` when it has bookings and every booking is earned.

| Figure | Definition |
|---|---|
| `keptMinor` | Σ `max0(funds)` over all bookings: customer money the business keeps (overpayments to give back are not revenue) |
| `earnedRevenueMinor` | Σ `max0(funds)` over earned bookings |
| `notYetEarnedMinor` | `keptMinor − earnedRevenueMinor` |
| `penaltiesKeptMinor` | Σ `max0(funds)` over cancelled bookings (a part of earned revenue: what a cancellation keeps; a penalty above what was collected is never charged, 6.4) |
| `refundedMinor`, `refundedByPurpose` | Σ succeeded refunds; split cancellation, price reduction, overpayment return, external (no purpose). Already out of `funds`: information, never subtracted again |
| `overpaymentsMinor`, `customerDebtMinor` | as 4.6; shown, not in the result |
| `feesMinor`, `feesImported` | Σ `payment_fees`; `feesImported` false when a succeeded payment went through a gateway that lists no settlements (4.6's rule): the panel says "fees not imported" |
| `expectedRevenueMinor` | Σ `totalMinor` of bookings in `deposit_paid`, `confirmed`, `balance_paid`, `completed` + Σ `penaltyMinor` of cancellations of bookings in `cancelled`/`refunded`. `approved` is not counted (it can still lapse) |
| `costCommittedMinor`, `costPaidMinor`, `costUnpaidMinor` | cost items of the trip in the trip currency, not `cancelled`: all, `paid` (active payment item), `planned`/`due` |
| `otherCurrencies[]` | per other currency C: `committedMinor`, `paidMinor`, `unpaidMinor` in C; `explicitPaidMinor` (items in C paid by a payment that meets rule 3) and `explicitDebitedMinor` (the bank amounts of those payments, in the trip currency); `leftOutMinor` = committed − explicitPaid in C (not in the result) |
| `costInResultMinor` | `costCommittedMinor + Σ explicitDebitedMinor` |
| `costPaidInResultMinor` | `costPaidMinor + Σ explicitDebitedMinor` |

Results (each says what it is; none is "the profit"):

- `resultCashMinor = keptMinor − feesMinor − costPaidInResultMinor`: money in less money out so far.
- `resultExpectedMinor = expectedRevenueMinor − feesMinor − costInResultMinor`: what the trip leaves if every booked amount is collected and every recorded cost stands. Fees are those so far.
- `resultEarnedMinor = earnedRevenueMinor − feesMinor − costInResultMinor` when the trip is settled, else `null` ("not final until every booking has returned or been called off").
- `complete` flags: `otherCurrencyLeftOut` (any `leftOutMinor` > 0), `feesNotImported`, `noSupplierCosts` (no cost at all: the result would be revenue only). The panel shows each as a warning next to the trip.

A trip with supplier payments and no customer money yet (a prepayment) is a row, as in 4.6. Trips are listed by trip name and currency; a trip is never combined with another.

### 3.3 Totals, costs with no trip, and the check against the ledger

- `totals[]` per currency: the sum of each money column of the rows of that currency (sums of per-trip figures; no figure of one trip is ever used to compute another's). `resultEarnedMinor` totals only settled trips and says how many.
- `unlinked[]`: cost with no trip, per currency (cost, paid, unpaid), exactly 4.6's; shown after the totals, never inside a trip.
- `reconciliation[]` per currency, each `{ reported, ledger, matches }` (the criterion "cuadra con el libro de pagos y con los costes registrados"): (1) customer money: Σ rows (`funds + over`, raw, unclamped) against the ledger's credit balance of the two accounts in that currency; (2) fees: Σ rows against `payment_fees`; (3) supplier paid: Σ (trips + other-currency lists + unlinked) against the debit balance of `supplier_payable`; (4) costs: Σ (trips + other-currency + unlinked, not cancelled) against `cost_items` read on its own. The pure `reconcile()` is table-tested with a deliberately wrong figure.
- `fx` (rule 5), the `fx_conversion` read.

### 3.4 Penalties and refunds

Penalties are `Cancellation.penaltyMinor` (6.4). What is kept is in `penaltiesKeptMinor`; what is still owed shows in the forecast as `penalties`. Refunds are only information in the result because `funds` is already net of them (a refund posts to `customer_funds`); a test (`refund_lowers_kept_not_double_counted`) refunds part of a payment and checks that `keptMinor` falls by that amount and `refundedMinor` rises by it, and the result moves once.

## 4. Cash forecast

`GET /admin/payments/reports/cash-forecast?from&to&currency` (permission `payments.report.cashForecast`; default window today to +90 days, at most two years like the 6.2 schedule). Pure core `core/cash-forecast.ts`, service `core/cash-forecast.service.ts`.

**Money in (customers).** For every booking with active plan items, the unpaid part of each item comes from the same `allocate()` the payment plan uses, fed with what the booking has collected and not promised back (`bookingCash.allocatableMinor`, read for all bookings in one SQL). Only the unpaid remainder is expected, on the item's due date. Three kinds: `instalments` (bookings in `deposit_paid`, `confirmed`, `balance_paid`), `tentative` (booking `approved`, deposit unpaid and still able to lapse), `penalties` (a cancelled booking's `penalty` item). Other statuses expect nothing.

**Money out (suppliers).** Unpaid cost items (`planned`, `due`, amount above 0, supplier not deleted) on their due date, in their own currency; no date goes to `undated`. Held allotments with a release date are decisions, not debts: not in the forecast (they stay in 6.2's schedule).

**Buckets**, per currency: `overdue` (due before today in Brisbane), one row per date in the window, `later` (after the window), `undated` (suppliers only). Overdue and undated are always listed, whatever the window. Customer instalments overdue are shown as overdue money in, not as certain.

**Net.** Per date: `firmNetMinor = instalmentsInMinor − supplierOutMinor`, and `runningNetMinor` is the sum of the dates up to that one, starting at 0 at the start of the window (the app holds no bank balance, and the panel says so). `tentative` and `penalties` are separate columns **not** in the net: they are less certain, so the net is the cautious one. Overdue and undated are not in the running figure either.

**Kept apart, never netted:** `customerDebtMinor` (the ledger receivable), `toGiveBackMinor` (overpayments the business holds for customers) and `refundsInFlightMinor` (app refunds requested or pending) are per-currency lines shown beside the forecast and never added to or taken from any column. Money customers paid for a trip is not offset against that trip's or any other trip's supplier payments: the per-currency timeline is a cash view of the business; `trips[]` repeats the same figures per trip (no-trip costs as their own row), so each trip's in and out can be read without any other trip's.

## 5. Accountant export

`POST /admin/payments/exports/accountant` with body `{ dataset: 'trip_result' | 'ledger' | 'supplier_payments', from, to }` (strict zod; dates are Brisbane calendar dates, range at most 366 days; `trip_result` ignores the dates but they are recorded). Permission `payments.export.accountant` with `requiresStepUp: true`, never an API key (all new permissions are owner-only until Oscar assigns them, as 4.6). A POST with `Cache-Control: no-store`, `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`, an attachment with a safe file name, and a per-user and per-IP hourly limit (10 and 100, like 7.2).

- **`trip_result`**: one row per trip and currency, the columns of section 3 (money as decimals in the trip's currency with its decimal places) plus a text column for other-currency costs ("EUR 120.00 recorded, EUR 80.00 paid; not converted").
- **`ledger`**: one row per ledger entry of the mode whose Brisbane date is in the range: date, transaction key (`payment:<id>:succeeded` ... no free text), kind, account, currency, debit, credit, booking reference (random, `@nopii`) and trip. No customer, no payer, no reference typed by staff.
- **`supplier_payments`**: one row per supplier payment in the range (`paidOn`), recorded and voided: date, supplier (a business name), currency, amount, status, bank amount and currency when typed, implied rate (rule 4, information), trip when all its items are of one trip. No `reference`, no `voidReason`, no cost description.

Every file starts with the byte order mark and has the header row in Australian English ("Date (Brisbane)", "Currency", "Debit", "Credit"), a column `Environment` (`test` or `live`) so test data cannot be mistaken for real, one value per cell, **money as an exact decimal string** produced from `bigint` and the currency's decimal places (`1234.50`, JPY `1234`), never a float. Text goes through the platform `cell()` (formula guard on every fragment and quoting, 7.fix-5); money cells are written raw only after a strict pattern check (`^-?\d+(\.\d+)?$`), which a spreadsheet reads as a number and cannot run (a negative amount is not a formula). More than 50 000 rows is a 422 `export_too_large` ("choose fewer dates"); an empty range is a file with only the header.

**Audit** in the same transaction as the read: `exports.accountant.generated`, entity `AccountantExport` with a fresh id, after: dataset, range, mode, row count, the currencies in the file; classes `nopii`. No values, no names. No entry, no file (the read and the audit write are one transaction; a failed audit rolls back and nothing is sent).

## 9. Panel, copy and the JavaScript budget

One admin page `/admin/trip-results` ("Trip results") with three sections: **Results by trip** (table per currency, warnings, totals, the unlinked costs, the ledger check, the `fx_conversion` read), **Cash forecast** (date range filter, one table per currency, the lines kept apart, a short "no bank balance" note) and **Accountant export** (dataset, dates, a button that asks for a fresh code through the existing step-up prompt and downloads the file). Semantic tables with captions and `scope`, labelled inputs, visible focus, errors in an `aria-live` status (existing `Status`), no colour-only meaning, keyboard-only flow. All copy in Australian English from the translation layer; no arithmetic in the browser.

**Public bundle budget.** `public-site-quality.spec.ts` allows 200 000 bytes of compressed JavaScript per page and the package builder is about 75 bytes under it, because the whole en-AU catalogue (131 kB of it the `admin` namespace) is imported by `i18n.ts` and so by every client component. The limit is not raised and no copy is trimmed. Instead the admin catalogue leaves the public JavaScript:

- `messages/en-AU.json` loses the `admin` key; it moves to `messages/en-AU.admin.json` (same keys, same text).
- `i18n.ts` keeps the public catalogues and gains `registerMessages(locale, tree)`; `lookup` and `messageCatalogue` read the public tree then the registered ones.
- `lib/i18n-admin.ts` imports the admin tree, registers it, and re-exports `t` and friends. Every admin module (the files under `components/admin`, `app/admin` and the panel libraries that use `admin.*` keys) imports `t` from `@/lib/i18n-admin`, so the tree is registered before any module-level `t()` call in that module. Public pages never import it.
- Vitest registers it in a setup file so existing tests, which read `admin.*` keys through `t()` or the raw catalogue, stay as they are; the three tests that import the JSON file directly read the two files.
- A convention test asserts that nothing outside the admin modules imports `i18n-admin` or the admin JSON, and that `i18n.ts` does not.

This is a mechanical move (no text changes). Expected effect: about 25 kB less compressed JavaScript on every page that ships client code.
