# Plan 5.5: privacy — export and erase a customer's data; separate marketing consent

Risk: high (personal data, permissions, append-only tables, concurrency with bookings, payments and the waiting list). Money is parked: nothing here moves money or computes an amount; the ledger does not exist yet (4.x), so "keep the ledger" means: never delete or rewrite an append-only or financial row (payments, snapshots, inventory movements, terms acceptances, audit log) and keep bookings and payments with the customer anonymised. Row: "Privacidad: exportar y borrar los datos de un cliente; consentimiento de marketing separado." Phase criterion: "Borrar un cliente elimina o anonimiza sus datos personales y conserva los asientos del libro de pagos." Depends on 5.1 (in `main`, 46ae34c). Reference: plan 0.1 sections 3.2, 4.4, 4.9, 4.10 and the 5.5 test list; `docs/web.md` (separate marketing consent, one-click unsubscribe); `docs/done/2.4.md`, `2.6.md`, `3.5.md`, `5.1.md`, `5.3.md`, `6.5.md`, `7.4.md`, `7.6.md`; `.claude/rules/*`.

## 1. What exists and what is missing

Exists: `EntityRegistry` (`platform/entities`) with `customerLink` and `export`/`anonymise` handlers registered by Customer, Booking, BookingTraveller, BookingSnapshot, BookingRoom, BookingExtra, InventoryClaim (bookings), Payment, PaymentSchedule, PaymentScheduleItem, TermsAcceptance, WebhookEvent, PaymentLink (payments), Quote (crm), Complaint (complaints), WaitlistEntry (waitlist), AlertSubscription (alerts). The audit service fills `AuditPiiValue.subjectCustomerId/subjectUserId` from the registry. Role `mt_privacy` (NOLOGIN) owns `privacy_purge_expired()`; `audit_pii_values` accepts `DELETE` only from `mt_privacy`. Customers are never deleted (trigger). `ownsBookingReference` and every customer-side flow (booking request, quote acceptance, waitlist offer, payment link) lock the customer `FOR UPDATE` first and refuse an anonymised one. Mail: `EmailSuppression` (HMAC only), marketing mail is checked against it at send time and gets a `List-Unsubscribe` header pointing at `/unsubscribe/<emailId>.<hmac>`, a route that does not exist yet (5.3 note). Automations (7.4) skip customer emails to suppressed addresses.

Missing: the erasure executor, `privacy_erase_customer`, the export, `PrivacyRequest`, `ConsentRecord`, the unsubscribe route, the endpoints, the panel and account screens, the registry-completeness test and the sweep. Registry gaps found by walking the schema: `InventoryMovement` and `PromoRedemption` reach `Booking` but are not registered; `OutboundEmail` has a `customerId` column without a relation; the polymorphic tables (`Note`, `Task`, `Tagging`, `SearchDocument`, `AuditLog`, `AutomationRun`) are not declared anywhere.

## 2. Scope decisions (conservative, reversible) and assumptions (instead of questions to Oscar)

1. **Erasure is staff-initiated, owner only, with step-up.** A customer can *ask* for it from `/account/privacy` (creates a `PrivacyRequest` and a system task for staff); only an owner executes it. Self-service erasure would turn a stolen inbox into a one-click wipe. Undo: add a customer route that calls the same executor.
2. **Erasure is refused (409 `erasure_rejected_with_active_trip`)** while the customer has a booking in `requested`, `approved`, `deposit_paid` or `confirmed` (any date: it can still take a payment or needs travellers), or `balance_paid` whose `returnDate` is today or later in Brisbane, or a payment in `created`, `pending` or `requires_action`. Staff cancel or close those first. Disputes do not exist yet (4.5 parked); their check is added with them. Same rule in TypeScript (for the 409) and in `privacy_erase_customer` (defence).
3. **Nothing append-only or financial is deleted or rewritten.** Bookings, snapshots, payments, schedules, inventory claims and movements, promo redemptions, terms acceptances and the audit log stay, linked to the anonymised customer; they hold no personal data by design (plan 0.1 4.9). The only deletions are rows with no value of their own: sessions, login tokens, alert subscriptions, search documents and the customer's `AuditPiiValue` rows (through `mt_privacy`).
4. **Exports are produced on demand and never stored** (no file, no `StoredFile`, no job), as JSON (`application/json`, attachment, `Cache-Control: no-store`). **Both exports (customer and owner) leave out staff notes and internal fields** (ids, hashes, tokens, provider references, versions), as the coordinator asked. Assumption to review: APP 12 can require giving staff notes on a formal access request; the owner reads them in the panel, and switching the owner export to include them is one argument (`audience: 'full'`, kept in the handlers for that reason). Recorded in `docs/done/5.5.md` for Oscar.
5. **Marketing consent is its own append-only record**, never created by terms acceptance, checkout or sign-in; default is *no consent*. Granted only from the signed-in account (the session came from a magic link to that address, which is the double confirmation of the address), withdrawn from the account or by the one-click unsubscribe. `ConsentRecord` keeps **no plain address** (only the HMAC, like `EmailSuppression`), so it needs no erasure exception (simpler than plan 0.1's `email` column, same evidential value). No checkbox is added to checkout in 5.5 (a new step in a money flow; the account is enough for the row).
6. **One-click unsubscribe = no marketing to that address.** It writes a withdrawal record and a `marketing` suppression (never downgrading an existing `all`). Opening alerts are marketing mail (5.3), so the suppression also stops them; the page says so. Granting consent again lifts only an `unsubscribed`/`marketing` suppression, never a bounce or complaint.
7. **No new feature flag**: privacy is a legal obligation, like complaints (core module, `flagKey: null`).
8. **Audit and events carry no personal data**: `privacy.customer.erased`, `privacy.export.generated`, `privacy.consent.changed` with ids and codes only; the outbox event `privacy.customer.erased` has `{ customerId }`.

## 3. Data model (migration `0027_privacy`, schema `privacy.prisma`)

- `PrivacyRequest`: `id`, `customerId` (FK customers, Restrict), `kind` export/erasure, `status` received/completed/rejected, `requestedBy` customer/staff, `requestedAt`, `completedAt?`, `rejectionCode?` (closed list: `active_trip`), `actorUserId?` (uuid, no FK). No free text (plan 0.1's `notes` dropped: nothing to redact). Partial unique index: one `received` erasure request per customer. Trigger: never deleted; only `status` (received → completed/rejected), `completedAt`, `rejectionCode`, `actorUserId` may change, once.
- `ConsentRecord` (`@appendOnly`): `id`, `customerId?` (FK customers, Restrict), `emailHash` (`@secret`, HMAC with `EMAIL_HASH_KEY`), `emailHashKeyVersion`, `purpose` (`marketing_email`), `granted`, `source` (account / unsubscribe_link / staff), `wordingKey` (code of the text shown, e.g. `marketing_email.v1`), `recordedAt`. CHECK: `customerId` required unless `source = 'unsubscribe_link'`. Trigger `mt_reject_modification` on UPDATE/DELETE/TRUNCATE, no exception for anyone. Current state = latest record (`recordedAt`, then `id`) for the customer or for its address hash.
- `privacy_erase_customer(p_customer uuid) RETURNS integer`, `SECURITY DEFINER`, owner `mt_privacy`, `SET search_path = pg_catalog, public, pg_temp`, objects schema-qualified, `EXECUTE` only for `mt_app`. Refuses unless the customer exists and is already anonymised (it runs last, inside the executor's transaction, so `mt_app` cannot use it to purge a live customer's audit values); repeats the active-trip rule of §2.2; deletes `audit_pii_values` whose subject is the customer or its user; writes a system `audit_logs` row with the count. `mt_privacy` gets `SELECT` on `customers`, `bookings`, `payments` (and keeps its existing grants). No other exception is added.

## 4. Registry contract (shared with 5.2 and every later module)

`EntityHandlers` keeps its two functions with optional extra arguments (existing handlers stay valid):
- `export(tx, customerId, opts?: { audience?: 'subject' | 'full' })`: rows of this customer, `subject` = what the customer may download (no staff notes or internal text); default `full`. The privacy export always asks for `subject` and then strips any `id`/`*Id` key (a `bookingId` becomes the booking's `reference`), any key containing hash/token/secret/ciphertext, and empty rows.
- `anonymise(tx, customerId, ctx?: { emails: readonly string[]; userId: string | null })`: idempotent, safe to run again; anonymises `@pii` columns (`[redacted]`, `{}`, NULL, `erased+<id>@invalid`, `ZZ`) or deletes rows with no value of their own; never touches append-only tables; `ctx.emails` are the customer's and its user's addresses read before anything changed (for rows linked only by address). It runs inside the executor's transaction with **Customer and the customer's Bookings and Payments already locked**; if it needs more locks it takes them in the order Booking → Payment → PaymentLink / waitlist entries / its own rows, by id.
- A module registers its tables in `onModuleInit` (as now). 5.2's `Traveller`: `customerLink: { kind: 'column', column: 'customerId' }`, handlers as above; its notes and search documents are handled generically (§5 step 7) once it is registered. A new table with a `customerId` column should also add the trigger `mt_customer_not_erased` (§5) if rows can be created without the customer lock.
- Test (`registry_covers_every_customer_table`): every model with a relation path to `Customer`, `Traveller`, `Booking`, `Quote` or `Complaint` (schema walk), and every model with a `customerId` column, is registered with handlers or is in the executor's own list (`User`, `Session`, `LoginToken`, `OutboundEmail`, `PrivacyRequest`, `ConsentRecord`); every model with `entityType`+`entityId` is in the polymorphic list with its treatment. Adds `InventoryMovement` (catalogue: export nothing, keep) and `PromoRedemption` (pricing: export code and dates, keep).

## 5. Erasure executor (`POST /admin/privacy/customers/:id/erasure`)

One `runTransaction` (READ COMMITTED; retried on 40001/40P01):
1. `SELECT … FROM customers WHERE id = $1 FOR UPDATE` → 404 if missing. Already anonymised → return the completed result (idempotent, nothing written).
2. Read `ctx` (customer email, user id, user email).
3. Lock the customer's bookings `ORDER BY id FOR UPDATE`, then its payments (by booking or payer) `ORDER BY id FOR UPDATE`.
4. Active-trip rule (§2.2) → 409, and a rejected-attempt audit row and `rejected` status for an open customer request in a separate transaction.
5. Every registered handler's `anonymise(tx, customerId, ctx)`, `Customer` last.
6. Platform steps: user → `erased+<userId>@invalid`, `disabled`, `anonymisedAt`; delete sessions and login tokens (by user or address); outbound emails (by `customerId` or address): `to` → `erased+<emailId>@invalid`, `subject` → `[redacted]` if not empty, `error` NULL, and a `queued` one becomes `suppressed` (`recipient_erased`), so a pending send never goes out (the send step locks the row and only sends `queued`).
7. Polymorphic: the ids of every registered entity of this customer (from the `customerLink` chain) → notes `[redacted]`, task title/description `[redacted]`, search documents deleted; taggings kept (no text); audit log and automation runs kept (no personal data by design).
8. `SELECT privacy_erase_customer($1)`.
9. `PrivacyRequest`: complete the open one or create a completed staff one; close its system task; audit `privacy.customer.erased` (no values); outbox `privacy.customer.erased`.

Defence in depth (migration): `BEFORE INSERT` trigger `mt_customer_not_erased` on `bookings`, `quotes`, `waitlist_entries`, `payments` (payer), `terms_acceptances`, `promo_redemptions`, `consent_records`: reads the customer `FOR KEY SHARE` (waits for an erasure in progress, then sees its result) and raises if `anonymisedAt` is set. Services already refuse; this closes any path that does not lock the customer first.

## 6. Export

- Customer: `GET /account/privacy/export`, `@OwnedBy({ kind: 'customer', resource: 'self' })`, rate-limited (5 per hour per user, 30 per IP; in memory, like documents), `Cache-Control: no-store`, attachment `marathon-trips-data-<yyyy-mm-dd>.json`. A customer whose record is anonymised or deleted gets 404.
- Owner: `GET /admin/privacy/customers/:id/export`, permission `privacy.customer.export` (step-up) and owner check, 10 per hour per staff user.
- Content: `{ format: 'marathon-trips.personal-data.v1', generatedAt, account: { email, createdAt, lastLoginAt }, sections: { <EntityType>: rows… }, marketingConsent: [ { granted, source, recordedAt } ], emails: [ { subject, category, status, sentAt } ], signIns: [ { createdAt, lastSeenAt, userAgent } ] }`, read in one REPEATABLE READ read-only transaction. Writes only a completed `PrivacyRequest` (kind export) and an audit row, both without personal data.

## 7. Marketing consent and unsubscribe

- `GET /account/privacy` → `{ marketingConsent: { granted, recordedAt }, erasureRequest: { status, requestedAt } | null }`; `PUT /account/privacy/marketing-consent { granted, wordingKey }` (customer locked first; a record only when the state changes; withdraw adds the suppression, grant lifts only an `unsubscribed` marketing one); `POST /account/privacy/erasure-request` (one open request; a system task "Privacy: erasure requested").
- `POST /unsubscribe/:ref` (`ref` = `<outboundEmailId>.<hmac>`), `@Public`, `@CsrfExempt` (protected by the HMAC; RFC 8058 one-click POST from mail clients), `@AuthRateLimit`. Wrong HMAC and unknown id: the same 404. Valid: withdrawal record (with `customerId` when the email has one) + marketing suppression, idempotent; an email already erased is a no-op 200. `GET /unsubscribe/:ref` changes nothing: 303 to the web page `/unsubscribe#ref=…` (fragment: the token never reaches a log), which has one button. Marketing mail gains `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`.
- Staff: `GET /admin/privacy/customers/:id` (permission `privacy.customer.read`): consent state and history (granted, source, date), requests, and whether erasure is possible now (with the code).

## 8. Permissions

`privacy.customer.read` (no step-up), `privacy.customer.export` and `privacy.customer.erase` (`requiresStepUp: true`). None is `apiKeyScopable`. Export and erase also check `isOwner` in the service, so a custom role that is given them still gets 403 `owner_only`.

## 9. Web

- `/account/privacy` (link from `/account`): consent checkbox, unticked unless the API says granted, with the wording; "Download my data"; "Ask us to delete my data" (and its status). All copy in `en-AU.json`.
- `/unsubscribe`: reads the fragment, one button, result message; no-JS fallback text.
- Panel, customer page: "Privacy" panel (consent state and history; Export and Erase buttons with step-up; erase asks to tick a confirmation and explains what is kept).

## 10. Tests (failure paths first)

1. Access: 401/403/404 matrix for every route; non-owner with a role holding the permissions → 403 `owner_only`; no step-up → 403 `step_up_required`; permissions not API-key scopable; `/account/privacy/*` take no ids (the owned-route walk).
2. `erasure_rejected_with_active_trip`: table-driven over every `BookingStatus` × departure/return today/yesterday/tomorrow (Brisbane), and live payment statuses; nothing changes (customer, notes, audit values, sessions intact).
3. Rollback: a handler throwing midway leaves everything as it was; then a retry succeeds.
4. Idempotent: second erasure 200, one audit row, one event; two concurrent erasures (barrier after the first locks the customer) → both 200, one audit row.
5. Concurrency with a barrier forcing the interleaving: (a) erasure holds the customer, a booking request for the same customer waits and then gets 409 `customer_closed`, no booking; (b) a payment-link start holds the booking, erasure waits and then 409 (payable booking), no change; (c) a waitlist promotion is offering, erasure waits, ends withdrawn with nothing held; (d) a mail send in progress, erasure waits and then redacts; a queued email is never sent after erasure.
6. Database: `mt_app` cannot `DELETE` audit values, `UPDATE`/`DELETE` consent records or privacy requests; `privacy_erase_customer` refuses a live customer and an active trip; it is owned by `mt_privacy` with a fixed `search_path` and `EXECUTE` only for `mt_app`; the insert trigger refuses a booking for an erased customer.
7. **Sweep** (`erasure_sweep_leaves_no_marker`): a customer whose every personal field holds a marker (names, phone, email, notes and tasks on customer, booking and complaint, complaint text, waitlist entry, alert subscription, outbound email, audit values from audited changes, sessions with user agent, login token) is erased; then every table of `public` and `pgboss` is scanned (`row::text ILIKE '%marker%'`) and the marker is found nowhere; another customer's marker is untouched; payments, snapshots, inventory movements and terms acceptances still exist (counts equal).
8. `registry_covers_every_customer_table` (§4).
9. Export: only the caller's data; no staff notes; no `id`/`*Id`/hash/token keys; no other customer's marker; `no-store` and attachment headers; 429 after the limit; no file, job or stored row besides the request and audit; anonymised customer 404.
10. Consent: default none; accepting terms at checkout creates no consent record; grant/withdraw idempotent; withdraw suppresses marketing mail at send and automations skip; grant lifts only `unsubscribed`; existing `all` never downgraded; unsubscribe link: bad HMAC = unknown = 404, GET changes nothing, POST twice = one record; `ConsentRecord` immutable even for `mt_owner`.
11. Web: unit tests for the account privacy page, the unsubscribe page and the panel (no strings outside `en-AU.json`, no ids shown).
