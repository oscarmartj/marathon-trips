# Plan 5.5: privacy — export and erase a customer's data; separate marketing consent

Risk: high (personal data, permissions, append-only tables, concurrency with bookings, payments and the waiting list). Money is parked: nothing here moves money or computes an amount; the ledger does not exist yet (4.x), so "keep the ledger" means: never delete or rewrite an append-only or financial row (payments, snapshots, inventory movements, terms acceptances, audit log) and keep bookings and payments with the customer anonymised. Row: "Privacidad: exportar y borrar los datos de un cliente; consentimiento de marketing separado." Phase criterion: "Borrar un cliente elimina o anonimiza sus datos personales y conserva los asientos del libro de pagos." Depends on 5.1 (in `main`, 46ae34c). Reference: plan 0.1 sections 3.2, 4.4, 4.9, 4.10, 6.3 and the 5.5 test list; `docs/web.md` (separate marketing consent, one-click unsubscribe); `docs/done/2.4.md`, `2.6.md`, `3.5.md`, `5.1.md`, `5.3.md`, `6.5.md`, `7.4.md`, `7.6.md`, `8.6.md`; `.claude/rules/*`.

Plan review (Opus, "aprobar con cambios", 17 points) is folded in; §13 maps each point to where it is handled.

## 1. What exists and what is missing

Exists: `EntityRegistry` (`platform/entities`) with `customerLink` and `export`/`anonymise` handlers registered by Customer, Booking, BookingTraveller, BookingSnapshot, BookingRoom, BookingExtra, InventoryClaim (bookings), Payment, PaymentSchedule, PaymentScheduleItem, TermsAcceptance, WebhookEvent, PaymentLink (payments), Quote (crm), Complaint (complaints), WaitlistEntry (waitlist), AlertSubscription (alerts). The audit service fills `AuditPiiValue.subjectCustomerId/subjectUserId` from the registry or a subject resolver. Role `mt_privacy` (NOLOGIN) owns `privacy_purge_expired()`; `audit_pii_values` accepts `DELETE` only from `mt_privacy`. Customers are never deleted (trigger). `ownsBookingReference` and the customer-side flows (booking request, quote acceptance, waitlist offer, payment link) lock the customer `FOR UPDATE` first and refuse an anonymised one. Mail: `EmailSuppression` (HMAC only), marketing mail is checked against it at send time and gets a `List-Unsubscribe` header pointing at `/unsubscribe/<emailId>.<hmac>`, a route that does not exist yet (5.3 note).

Missing or wrong: the erasure executor, `privacy_erase_customer`, the export, `PrivacyRequest`, `ConsentRecord`, the unsubscribe route, the endpoints and screens, the registry-completeness test and the sweep. Found while planning and in review: `InventoryMovement` and `PromoRedemption` reach `Booking` but are not registered; `OutboundEmail` has an unrelated `customerId`; the polymorphic tables are not declared; alert subscriptions made through the public route have no `customerId`, so the alerts handler erases nothing; `Task` audit values have no subject; Customer, Booking and Complaint exports contain staff notes; automations (7.4) treat a `marketing` suppression as a stop for transactional mail and `addSuppression` can downgrade `all` to `marketing`; complaints and automation mail read the customer unlocked, so personal data can be written after an erasure; a webhook matched after an erasure stores a raw body with payer data.

## 2. Scope decisions (conservative, reversible) and assumptions (instead of questions to Oscar)

1. **Erasure of a customer is staff-initiated, owner only, with step-up.** A customer can *ask* for it from `/account/privacy` (a `PrivacyRequest` and a system task for staff). Self-service erasure would turn a stolen inbox into a one-click wipe. Exception: a sign-in account **with no customer record** (no bookings, quotes, complaints or waiting list, nothing staff need) is erased by its owner from the account page, after a confirmation; it loses only its sign-in address, sessions, alerts and email log.
2. **Erasure is refused (409 `erasure_rejected_with_active_trip`, with a reason code)** by one SQL function `privacy_active_trip_reason(customer)` used by TypeScript and by `privacy_erase_customer`: `open_booking` (a booking in `requested`, `approved`, `deposit_paid`, `confirmed`, any date), `trip_not_finished` (`balance_paid` with `returnDate` today or later in Brisbane), `live_payment` (a payment of the customer or of its bookings in `created`, `pending`, `requires_action`), `refund_may_be_due` (a `cancelled`, `declined` or `expired` booking with a `succeeded` payment: refunds are parked, so money may be owed). Disputes do not exist yet (4.5 parked); their check is added with them. A 409 changes nothing, not even an open request's status.
3. **Nothing append-only or financial is deleted or rewritten.** Bookings, snapshots, payments, schedules, inventory claims and movements, promo redemptions, terms acceptances and the audit log stay, linked to the anonymised customer; they hold no personal data by design. Deleted: rows with no value of their own (sessions, login tokens, alert subscriptions, search documents, the customer's `AuditPiiValue` rows through `mt_privacy`). `ConsentRecord.customerId` is set to NULL by `mt_privacy` (plan 0.1 4.9 exception), after a final withdrawal record for the address.
4. **Exports are produced on demand and never stored** (no file, no `StoredFile`, no job), JSON, attachment, `Cache-Control: no-store`. **Audience `subject` is the default** everywhere: no staff notes, no internal fields. The customer download is always `subject`. The owner export is `subject` unless the owner asks for `?audience=full`, which adds the staff notes and tasks on the customer's records in a separate `staffNotes` section, for a formal access request (APP 12); the owner reviews it before sending. Recorded for Oscar.
5. **Marketing consent is its own append-only record**, never created by terms acceptance, checkout or sign-in; default is *no consent*. Granted only from the signed-in account (the session came from a magic link to that address: the double confirmation), withdrawn from the account or by one-click unsubscribe. `ConsentRecord` keeps no plain address (HMAC only, like `EmailSuppression`). No checkbox in checkout in 5.5.
6. **Suppression scopes.** One-click unsubscribe or withdrawal writes a `marketing` suppression: no marketing mail (including opening alerts, which 5.3 sends as marketing; the page says so). **Transactional mail is stopped only by an `all` suppression** (bounce, complaint): 7.4's automation step is changed accordingly (5.6 does the same in its reminders). A suppression is never downgraded from `all` to `marketing` (`addSuppression` fixed). Granting consent again lifts only an `unsubscribed`/`marketing` suppression.
7. **Accounts without a customer record** (review point 6): every `/account/privacy` route first locks the customer of the session's user, or links the staff-created customer with the verified address (the first half of the 2.4 helper, extracted as `CustomerService.lockForUser`); it does not create one, because a customer needs a name and country we do not have. Without one, consent is recorded by address hash only (`customerId` NULL) and the current state is read by customer **or** address hash, so it carries over when the customer is created; the export holds the account data; erasure is the account-only path of 2.1.
8. **No new feature flag**: privacy is a legal obligation, like complaints (core module, `flagKey: null`).
9. **Audit and events carry no personal data**: actions `privacy.customer.erased`, `privacy.account.erased`, `privacy.export.generated`, `privacy.erasure.requested`, `privacy.consent.changed`, ids and codes only; outbox `privacy.customer.erased` with `{ customerId }`.
10. **Complaints logged without a customer but with the customer's address** (`complainantEmail`, case-insensitive): exported with the customer's data and redacted on erasure, like the customer's own; the complaint row, dates, category, status and outcome stay (procedure evidence).
11. **Backups** (8.6): restoring an older backup brings erased customers back. The completed erasure requests are the ledger; before a restore the operator saves `pnpm api:cli privacy:erasures-list` (customer and user ids only) from the live database, and after it runs `pnpm api:cli privacy:erasures-reapply <file>`, which erases again every listed customer or account not anonymised in the restored data and lists those it cannot (active trip in the old data) for a manual decision. The API also logs one line per erasure with the id only, so the list can be rebuilt from logs if the live database is lost. Documented in `docs/deploy/guide.md`.

## 3. Data model (migration `0027_privacy`, schema `privacy.prisma`)

- `PrivacyRequest`: `id`, `customerId?` (FK customers, Restrict), `userId?` (uuid, no FK; account-only requests), `kind` export/erasure, `status` received/completed, `requestedBy` customer/staff, `requestedAt`, `completedAt?`, `actorUserId?` (uuid, no FK). CHECK: customer or user present. No free text. Partial unique index: one `received` erasure request per customer. Trigger: never deleted; only `received → completed` (with `completedAt`, `actorUserId`), once.
- `ConsentRecord` (`@appendOnly`): `id`, `customerId?` (FK customers, Restrict), `emailHash` (`@secret`), `emailHashKeyVersion`, `purpose` (`marketing_email`), `granted`, `source` (account / unsubscribe_link / erasure), `wordingKey` (code of the text shown), `recordedAt` (default `clock_timestamp()`). CHECK: `source = 'erasure' ⇒ NOT granted`. Trigger: rejects UPDATE, DELETE and TRUNCATE, except an UPDATE by `mt_privacy` that only sets `customerId` to NULL.
- Functions, all `SET search_path = pg_catalog, public, pg_temp`, schema-qualified, `REVOKE EXECUTE … FROM PUBLIC` explicitly:
  - `privacy_active_trip_reason(p_customer uuid) RETURNS text` (STABLE, owner `mt_owner`, `EXECUTE` for `mt_app` and `mt_privacy`): the rule of 2.2, NULL when erasure may proceed.
  - `privacy_erase_customer(p_customer uuid) RETURNS integer` (`SECURITY DEFINER`, owner `mt_privacy`, `EXECUTE` for `mt_app` only): refuses unless the customer exists and is already anonymised (it runs last, inside the executor's transaction, so `mt_app` cannot purge a live customer's audit values); repeats the active-trip rule; deletes `audit_pii_values` whose subject is the customer or its user, **and** subject-less values of audit rows about the customer's own records found by SQL (`Customer` itself, its `Booking`, `Quote`, `Complaint`, `WaitlistEntry` rows, and any `Task` on one of those; old rows included); sets `consent_records."customerId"` to NULL for the customer; writes a system `audit_logs` row with the count. 5.2 extends the subject-less part for `Traveller` with `CREATE OR REPLACE` if it needs to (its rows have a column link, so their values already carry the subject).
  - `privacy_erase_account(p_user uuid) RETURNS integer` (same security): only a `customer` user, already anonymised, with no customer row; deletes its `subjectUserId` values.
- `mt_customer_not_erased()` trigger function, `BEFORE INSERT` (and `BEFORE UPDATE OF "customerId"` where the column can change) on `bookings`, `quotes`, `waitlist_entries`, `payments` (payer), `terms_acceptances`, `promo_redemptions`, `consent_records`, `complaints`, `outbound_emails` (when `customerId` is set) and `travellers` (when the table exists: 5.2 adds it in 0026; if 5.5 merges first, 5.2 adds the trigger): reads the customer `FOR KEY SHARE` (waits for an erasure in progress, then sees its result) and raises `customer_erased`, mapped to 409 `customer_closed`.
- `Task` gets an audit subject resolver (through its `entityType`/`entityId` and the registry), so new task values carry the subject.

## 4. Registry contract (shared with 5.2 and every later module)

`EntityHandlers` keeps its two functions with optional arguments (existing handlers stay valid):
- `export(tx, customerId, opts?: { audience?: 'subject' | 'full'; emails?: readonly string[] })`: this customer's rows as the customer may see them. **Default and expected audience: `subject`.** Handlers never return staff notes (notes and tasks are polymorphic and exported by the privacy module for `full` only). The privacy export then strips any `id`/`*Id` key (a `bookingId` becomes the booking `reference`), any key containing hash/token/secret/ciphertext, and empty rows.
- `anonymise(tx, customerId, ctx?: { emails: readonly string[]; userId: string | null })`: idempotent; anonymises every `@pii` column (`[redacted]`, `{}`, NULL, `erased+<id>@invalid`, `ZZ`, a NULL date or number) or deletes rows with no value of their own; never touches append-only tables. `ctx.emails` are the customer's and its user's addresses, lower-cased, read before anything changed: handlers match rows linked only by address with `lower(column) = ANY(emails)`. It runs inside the executor's transaction with **Customer and the customer's Bookings already locked**; further locks follow plan 0.1 6.3 (Booking → Payment → PaymentLink / waitlist entries / own rows, by id).
- A module registers its tables in `onModuleInit`. 5.2's `Traveller`: `customerLink: { kind: 'column', column: 'customerId' }`; `anonymise` clears `dateOfBirth`, `estimatedFinishSeconds` and every text column; its notes, tasks and search documents are handled generically once registered; the sweep (§10.7) covers it automatically. A table with a `customerId` column adds `mt_customer_not_erased`.
- Test `registry_covers_every_customer_table`: every model with a relation path to `Customer`, `Traveller`, `Booking`, `Quote` or `Complaint`, and every model with a `customerId` column, is registered with handlers or is in the executor's own list (`User`, `Session`, `LoginToken`, `OutboundEmail`, `PrivacyRequest`, `ConsentRecord`); every model with `entityType`+`entityId` is in the polymorphic list with its treatment (`Note`, `Task` redacted; `SearchDocument` deleted; `Tagging`, `AuditLog`, `AutomationRun` kept, no text). Registers `InventoryMovement` (catalogue: nothing to export or erase) and `PromoRedemption` (pricing: code and dates; kept).

## 5. Erasure executor (`POST /admin/privacy/customers/:id/erasure`)

One `runTransaction` (READ COMMITTED, retried on 40001/40P01):
1. `SELECT … FROM customers WHERE id = $1 FOR UPDATE` → 404 if missing. Already anonymised → the completed result, nothing written (idempotent).
2. `ctx`: customer email, user id and user email (lower-cased).
3. Lock the customer's bookings `ORDER BY id FOR UPDATE`. Payments are **not** locked here (their writers lock the booking first; locking them now would invert Payment → waitlist entries in the waitlist handler).
4. `privacy_active_trip_reason` → 409 (rollback; nothing written but a separate audit row `privacy.erasure.rejected` with the code).
5. Final withdrawal `ConsentRecord` for each address (source `erasure`), then every registered handler's `anonymise(tx, customerId, ctx)`, `Customer` last.
6. Platform steps: user → `erased+<userId>@invalid`, `disabled`, `anonymisedAt`; delete sessions and login tokens (by user or `lower(email)`); outbound emails (by `customerId` or `lower("to")`): `to` → `erased+<emailId>@invalid`, `subject` → `[redacted]` if not empty, `error` NULL, and a `queued` one becomes `suppressed` (`recipient_erased`). The send step also refuses (`suppressed`, `recipient_erased`) an email whose customer is anonymised, read after it locks the row.
7. Polymorphic: the ids of every registered entity of this customer (walking each `customerLink`) → notes `[redacted]`, task title and description `[redacted]`, search documents deleted.
8. `SELECT privacy_erase_customer($1)`.
9. `PrivacyRequest`: complete the open one or create a completed staff one; close its system task; audit `privacy.customer.erased` (no values); outbox `privacy.customer.erased`; log line with the id.

Account-only erasure (2.1): same transaction shape keyed by the user: lock the user `FOR UPDATE`, confirm it has no customer, steps 5 (consent by address), 6 and `privacy_erase_account`, alert subscriptions by address through the alerts handler's address path, completed `PrivacyRequest` with `userId`, the session cookie cleared.

Webhooks (payments, review point 9): when an event is matched to a payment, the same update stores `rawBody = NULL` if the booking's customer is anonymised.

## 6. Export

- Customer: `GET /account/privacy/export`, `@OwnedBy({ kind: 'customer', resource: 'self' })`, rate-limited (5 per hour per user, 30 per IP; in memory, like documents), `Cache-Control: no-store`, attachment `marathon-trips-data-<yyyy-mm-dd>.json`.
- Owner: `GET /admin/privacy/customers/:id/export[?audience=full]`, permission `privacy.customer.export` (step-up) and owner check, 10 per hour per staff user.
- Content: `{ format: 'marathon-trips.personal-data.v1', generatedAt, account: { email, createdAt, lastLoginAt }, sections: { <EntityType>: rows… }, marketingConsent: [ { granted, source, recordedAt } ], emails: [ { subject, category, status, sentAt } ], signIns: [ { createdAt, lastSeenAt, userAgent } ], staffNotes?: [ … ] }`. Read in a read-only REPEATABLE READ transaction; then, in a separate transaction, a completed `PrivacyRequest` (kind export) and an audit row, both without personal data.

## 7. Marketing consent and unsubscribe

- `GET /account/privacy` → `{ hasCustomerRecord, marketingConsent: { granted, recordedAt }, wordingKey, erasureRequest: { status, requestedAt } | null }`.
- `PUT /account/privacy/marketing-consent { granted, wordingKey }`: `wordingKey` must equal the server's current key (`MARKETING_WORDING_KEY`, shared) or 422; customer locked (2.7), then `pg_advisory_xact_lock` on the address hash; a record only when the state changes; withdrawal adds the `marketing` suppression (never downgrading), grant lifts only an `unsubscribed`/`marketing` one.
- `POST /account/privacy/erasure-request`: with a customer, one open request and a system task; without one, the account-only erasure (2.1) after a `confirm: true` body.
- `POST /unsubscribe/:ref` (`ref` = `<outboundEmailId>.<hmac>`), `@Public`, `@CsrfExempt` (HMAC-protected; RFC 8058 one-click POST from mail clients), `@AuthRateLimit`, exempt from maintenance mode (added to both exact exemption lists in the tests). Wrong HMAC and unknown id: the same 404. Valid: same advisory lock, withdrawal record (with the email's `customerId` if any and the customer is not anonymised) and `marketing` suppression; idempotent; an erased email is a no-op 200. `GET /unsubscribe/:ref` changes nothing: 303 to `/unsubscribe#ref=…` (fragment: the token never reaches a log), which has one button. Marketing mail gains `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`.
- Staff: `GET /admin/privacy/customers/:id` (permission `privacy.customer.read`): consent state and history, requests, and whether erasure is possible now (reason code).

## 8. Permissions

`privacy.customer.read` (no step-up), `privacy.customer.export` and `privacy.customer.erase` (`requiresStepUp: true`). None is `apiKeyScopable`. Export and erase also check `isOwner` in the service: a custom role given them still gets 403 `owner_only`.

## 10. Tests (failure paths first)

1. Access: 401/403/404 matrix; non-owner with a role holding the permissions → 403 `owner_only`; no step-up → 403 `step_up_required`; not API-key scopable; `/account/privacy/*` take no ids (owned-route walk); `/unsubscribe` in the CSRF and maintenance exemption lists.
2. `erasure_rejected_with_active_trip`: table-driven over every `BookingStatus` × return/departure yesterday/today/tomorrow (Brisbane) × with/without a succeeded payment, and live payment statuses; nothing changes; an open request stays `received`.
3. Rollback: a handler throwing midway leaves everything as it was; a retry then succeeds.
4. Idempotent: second erasure 200, one audit row, one event; two concurrent erasures (barrier after the first locks the customer) → both 200, one audit row; `erasing_two_customers_succeeds`.
5. Concurrency with a barrier forcing the interleaving: (a) a booking request waits on the customer and gets 409 `customer_closed`, no booking; (b) a payment-link start holds the booking, erasure waits then 409 (payable booking); (c) a waitlist promotion is offering, erasure ends with the entry withdrawn and nothing held; (d) a mail send holds its email row (and may lock a booking): the outcome is a wait or a retried deadlock, never an unredacted row or a send after the erasure; (e) a complaint creation read the customer before the erasure and inserts after it → 409, no personal data written; (f) an automation email read the address before the erasure and enqueues after → refused by the trigger, nothing sent.
6. Database: `mt_app` cannot delete audit values, change consent records or delete privacy requests; the `mt_privacy` exception on consent records only allows `customerId → NULL`; `privacy_erase_customer` refuses a live customer and an active trip; owners, `search_path`, `EXECUTE` grants (none for `PUBLIC`) checked from the catalogue; the insert triggers refuse an erased customer.
7. **Sweep** (`erasure_sweep_leaves_no_marker`), generated, not hand-listed: for every registered model with a customer link and every model of the executor's own list, the customer's rows (found through the link chain) get, as `mt_app` and one savepoint per column, a marker in every `@pii` (or unannotated) text, `String[]`, `Json` and `Bytes` column and a sentinel in every `@pii` date, integer and `Char(n)` column (columns whose constraints refuse a value are reported and skipped); plus notes and tasks on customer, booking and complaint, audited changes, a public-route alert subscription with a mixed-case address, an outbound email, a session and a login token. After the erasure every table of `public` and `pgboss` is scanned (`row::text ILIKE '%marker%'`) and the marker is nowhere; sentinels are gone from the customer's rows; another customer's marker is untouched; payments, snapshots, movements and acceptances keep their counts. `erasure_redacts_polymorphic_notes_and_tasks` and `erasure_removes_user_audit_pii_written_before_customer_existed`.
8. `registry_covers_every_customer_table` (§4).
9. Export: notes seeded on Customer, Booking and Complaint are absent from `subject` and present only in `full` `staffNotes`; no `id`/`*Id`/hash/token keys; no other customer's marker; headers; 429 after the limit; nothing stored besides the request and audit; account-only export.
10. Consent and suppression: default none; terms acceptance creates no consent record; grant/withdraw idempotent; unknown `wordingKey` 422; withdrawal stops marketing at send; `marketing_withdrawal_keeps_balance_reminders` (automation transactional email still sent with a `marketing` suppression, skipped with `all`); `addSuppression` never downgrades; grant lifts only `unsubscribed`; unsubscribe: bad HMAC = unknown = 404, GET changes nothing, POST twice = one record; unsubscribe and consent PUT racing on one address end in a consistent state; account without customer: consent by hash carries over to the customer created later.
11. Webhook matched after erasure stores no raw body.
12. Web: unit tests for the account privacy page, the unsubscribe page and the panel (copy from `en-AU.json`, no ids shown).
